# TLA+ models of byoa's concurrent protocols

These models check the parts of byoa where several backends, sandboxes and clocks act on one user's state at the same time. TLC, the TLA+ model checker, tries every order of their steps in small worlds (two jobs, two backends), with backends that die, leases that run out, and clocks that differ. This is check V22 in [SPEC.md](../../SPEC.md#verification).

```sh
spec/tla/check.sh            # every model, about 25 minutes on 2 cores
spec/tla/check.sh LoginLease # the models whose file names contain "LoginLease"
```

`check.sh` needs Java 11 or later. It downloads `tla2tools.jar` 1.7.4 the first time, and checks its SHA-256. Each model in `models/` is `<Spec>_<name>.cfg`. Its first line says what TLC must find: `\* expect: ok` (no error), or `\* expect: violated <Property>` (a counterexample that TLC must still find: an open issue, or the code before a fix). Its second line says why. The CI job `.github/workflows/tla.yml` runs them all when a model or the code it models changes.

## The models

| Spec | What it models | Properties |
|---|---|---|
| [JobTiming.tla](JobTiming.tla) | The jobs of one kept sandbox over time, in steps of 15 s: the slot's lease (`slots.ts`), resume and pause, setup, the wait for the AI login, the login watcher, checkpoints, `close`, the E2B sandbox timer (`timeoutMs`, pause on timeout, `STOP_ALL` on resume), and backends that die (`job.ts`, `login-stored.ts`, `sandbox/e2b.ts`) | `OneJobPerSandbox` (R5.4, R5.9), `NoLoginInPausedSandbox` (R1.4), `HarnessGetsItsTime`, `WatcherSparesHarness`, `NoLostRefresh` |
| [LoginLease.tla](LoginLease.tla) | The stored login of one user and harness, in steps of about a minute: `take` (the login, the lease, compare-and-set, `want`, the second read of the login's version), `give`, the line (`queue.ts`), `load` with the loan record, checkpoint, `release`, `accept`, a sign-in again (to the same or another account), and the lab, which rotates the refresh token at each refresh. Backends die, and their clocks may be ahead (`Skew`). | `NoRefreshRace` (R1.4: never two CLIs refreshing one login), `StoreNeverRegresses` (a save never puts an older login, or another account's, over the stored one), `LoginWorks` (no refresh token is lost), `BoundedWait` (a job in line takes the login within the time of the jobs before it) |
| [SignIn.tla](SignIn.tla) | `connectLogin`, `finishLogin` (a check-in), `cancelLogin` and `signOut` of one user, from two backends at the same time, and the provider's timer of each sign-in sandbox (`login.ts`) | `Cap` (R1.7: 15 minutes at most), `CancelEnds`, `SignOutWins` |
| [Index.tla](Index.tla) | One entry and its index (`usersandboxes/`, `conversations/`) while a job writes it and the app deletes it (`slots.ts` take and remove; `conversation.ts` finish and remove) | `NoOrphan`: every entry is in its index, so `deleteSandbox` and `deleteConversation` find it |

## What TLC found

1. **Fixed: two jobs in one kept sandbox.** A job took its slot's lease (the job timeout + 5 minutes), and then waited in line for the AI login, with no time limit. When the wait was longer than the lease, the lease ran out while the job still had the sandbox, and the next job of that slot took it and resumed the same sandbox. On E2B that resume runs `STOP_ALL`, which ends the first job's harness; on the other providers, two jobs ran in one sandbox and one `~/work`. This needs `parallel: false`, or a login that is due for a refresh, and jobs that hold the login longer than about 5 minutes. Now a job renews its slot's lease every minute until its harness starts (`job.ts`, `slots.renew`). Models: `JobTiming_main_slot` (118baf4), `JobTiming_heartbeat_slot*` (the fix). Test: "a job that waits in line for the AI login keeps its sandbox" in `test/login.test.ts`, which fails on 118baf4.
2. **Fixed: the job's time started before the wait for the login.** `job.ts` computed the watcher's deadline (now + job timeout + 45 s) before `load` waited in line for the login, and E2B's sandbox timer (job timeout + 60 s) started at resume, while the harness got its whole timeout from when it started. So after a wait for the login:
   - the watcher removed the login files while the harness still ran: after a wait longer than the job timeout + 45 s, about 1 s after it started (`JobTiming_heartbeat_early`);
   - on E2B the sandbox paused before the job's time ended (`JobTiming_heartbeat_cut`);
   - after a setup longer than 15 s, or any wait for the login, E2B paused the sandbox before the watcher ran, so a job whose backend died left the login files in the paused sandbox, against R1.4 (`JobTiming_heartbeat_watcher`).

   Now the job's time starts when it has the login (`TimeFromLogin` in the model, checked in `JobTiming_fixed_*`): `job.ts` sets the sandbox's time again with the new optional `Sandbox.setTimeout` (E2B; Daytona for a sandbox of one job), and `load` adds the time it waited to the watcher's deadline. Test: the same test in `test/login.test.ts` checks the sandbox's time and the watcher after a 36-minute wait. Instabox cannot change a sandbox's time after it starts, so there a sandbox of one job (`sandboxPer: 'job'`) that waited long for the login can still end before the job's time.
3. **Fixed: a deleted conversation's transcript, or a deleted slot, could stay.** `deleteConversation` read the index, deleted each record, then took them out of the index. A job of that conversation that saved its record between the delete and the index update put the record back, and no index listed it, so a later `deleteConversation` (also when the app deletes the user) never found it. A backend that died between `finish`'s put and its index update left one too. The slot index had the same race: a job that started during `deleteSandbox` or `signOut` created a slot that no index listed, so its sandbox was never deleted (`Index_*_main`, `Index_conversation_crash`). Now the writer adds the entry to the index before and after it writes it, and `remove`, after its index update, reads each entry again and lists one that a job wrote meanwhile (`Index_*_fixed`). `remove` keeps its order, so a remove that fails part way (a provider error) can run again. A backend that dies in its write while a remove runs can still leave one entry (`Index_*_fixed_crash`); a store with no transactions cannot rule that out. Test: "deleteConversation while a job of that conversation saves it" in `test/fake.test.ts`.
4. **Open, small: sign-in calls that overlap.** A `signOut` that runs while `finishLogin` saves the login leaves the user signed in, with the login stored, after `signOut` returned (`SignIn_signout`; a scratch test against the code showed it). A `cancelLogin` that runs while `connectLogin` creates its sandbox does not end that sandbox; it ends 2 minutes later, as one with no check-in (`SignIn_cancel`). Both need the app to make the two calls at the same time.
5. **Holds.** In every model with no error: one job alone holds a login that is due, a shared login is never refreshed by two CLIs, no save puts an older login or another account's over the stored one (also with the loan record of a dead job, and a sign-in again in between), the line serves jobs in order, and a sign-in sandbox never outlives its 15 minutes while its backend lives.
6. **Known limits, as SPEC.md says.** A dead backend loses a refresh that its CLI made after the last checkpoint (`JobTiming_fixed_crash_loses`, `LoginLease_crash_lost`). A CLI that refreshes early, after a 401 (`LoginLease_early401`), or a backend clock more than the lease's margin ahead (`LoginLease_skew_large`, 3 steps with a margin of 2: the 5 minutes), can make two CLIs refresh one login. A clock 1 step ahead is safe (`LoginLease_skew_small`). A backend that dies between `finishLogin`'s resume and its `keepAlive` leaves the sign-in sandbox up to 2 minutes + `waitMs` past its 15 minutes (`SignIn_cap_crash`).

## Where the models differ from the code

Each spec's header lists its own. In short:

- Small worlds: two jobs, two backends, one user, and short times. The models keep the 60 s and 45 s margins of the code, and scale the job's time, the 5-minute margin and the periods down, so that TLC can check them; what matters is how the times order.
- A step of a live backend (a store call, a sandbox call) takes less than one step of time. A real backend can stall longer, as when an event loop blocks; that is like a clock that is behind.
- The login files are one record (account, refresh token, refresh time, due time). `accept` compares the account and the time, as the code does. The CLI's own behavior (when it refreshes, that it rotates the refresh token) is from the comments in `harness/*.ts`.
- The store is linearizable, and its versions never repeat for a key (Postgres: one sequence; Convex: the row id; `memoryStore`: one counter). The models write no other store, so they assume nothing about lost or reordered writes; an adapter whose `cas` is not atomic breaks these results.
- Left out: steering (`job.send` and the drivers; how each CLI takes a message during a turn is the CLI's, so a model would only restate our guess), MCP grant refresh (R2.3), job tokens, plan keys, and the volume login (`cliLogin` jobs).
